## The 12 hook archetypes (slide 1)

Pick ONE. Adapt with named numbers + named villain.

### 1. Contrarian punch
**Template:** "Everyone is X. They're <em>building the wrong thing</em>."
**When:** You have a strong opinion + are willing to lose followers.
**Real example:** "Every founder is hiring AI engineers. They're solving the wrong problem."

### 2. Specific number + outcome
**Template:** "We shipped N Y in W weeks. <em>Here's the stack.</em>"
**When:** You have receipts. Best for case-study decks.
**Real example:** "We shipped 7 client AI agents in 6 weeks. Here's the stack."

### 3. Mistake confession
**Template:** "I burned $X on Y nobody used. <em>N lessons.</em>"
**When:** Vulnerability + lessons. Drives saves.
**Real example:** "I burned $11k on AI agents nobody used. 4 lessons."

### 4. Curiosity gap
**Template:** "The X most Y will <em>quietly kill</em> in 2026."
**When:** You want swipe-through > engagement.
**Real example:** "The agent layer most creative tools will quietly kill in 2026."

### 5. Listicle promise
**Template:** "N X you'll <em>actually use</em>."
**When:** Save-bait listicles. The gold standard for IG.
**Real example:** "9 AI agents creative teams will actually use."

### 6. Question hook
**Template:** "Why does X keep failing <em>inside Y</em>?"
**When:** Educational + Reddit-style.
**Real example:** "Why does ChatGPT keep failing inside Adobe workflows?"

### 7. Identity callout
**Template:** "If you're a [role] in 2026, <em>read this first.</em>"
**When:** Targeting a specific niche audience. Kills broad reach but spikes save rate from target.
**Real example:** "If you're a creative director adding AI in 2026, read this first."

### 8. Status reveal
**Template:** "How X are quietly replacing Y — <em>in N slides.</em>"
**When:** Trend commentary.
**Real example:** "How AI agents are quietly replacing your media buyer — in 6 slides."

### 9. Before/after compression
**Template:** "From [old metric] to <em>[new metric].</em>"
**When:** Workflow transformation stories.
**Real example:** "From 14-day storyboard cycles to 38 minutes."

### 10. Enemy-Hero-Teaser
**Template:** "[Enemy] is dying. [Hero] is <em>eating it.</em>"
**When:** Bold market commentary.
**Real example:** "Agency retainers are dying. Embedded AI is eating them."

### 11. Receipt drop
**Template:** Slack/DM screenshot: "Can we automate this?" + carousel of how
**When:** You have a real screenshot. Highest authenticity signal.

### 12. Naming-the-thing
**Template:** "There's a name for X: <em>'[term].'</em>"
**When:** You can coin/popularize a term.
**Real example:** "There's a name for AI tools nobody uses: 'demoware.'"

**Hook quality test:** if you can't paste it into Twitter and have it land as a single tweet, it's not a hook yet. Iterate before building.

## Slide-2 patterns (the swipe-survival slide)

60-70% of viewers bail or commit on slide 2. Pick ONE based on the hook archetype.

### Agitate the pain
"And it's worse than you think. Here's what [Y] actually costs you: [stat]."
**Pairs with:** Mistake confession (#3), Question hook (#6), Status reveal (#8)
**Example:** "Most creative teams lose 11 hrs/week to revisions an agent could absorb."

### Reframe the villain
"The problem isn't [obvious]. It's [unseen lever]."
**Pairs with:** Contrarian punch (#1), Curiosity gap (#4)
**Example:** "The problem isn't the model. It's the workflow you wrapped around it."

### Set the stakes
"Skip this and [consequence]. By [date], [outcome]."
**Pairs with:** Identity callout (#7), Enemy-Hero-Teaser (#10)
**Example:** "Ignore agent layers in 2026 and your studio is bidding against software, not agencies."

### Promise the payoff
"By slide [N] you'll know [specific thing]."
**Pairs with:** Listicle promise (#5), Specific number (#2)
**Example:** "By slide 7 you'll have the exact 4-agent stack we use for client onboarding."

### List intro
"Here are the [N]. Number [X] is the one nobody copies."
**Pairs with:** Listicle promise (#5)
**Example:** "Here are 7 AI agents we ship inside Adobe. #4 is the one most teams skip."

## Mid-carousel patterns (slides 3 to N-1)

| Pattern | Template | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Step-by-step** | "Step [N]: [verb]. [Why in one line]." + terminal block / icon | Tactical how-to |
| **Numbered list item** | "[#]. [Bold claim] — [proof number]." | Save-bait educational |
| **Before / After** | "Before: [their words]. After: [metric]." | Case studies |
| **Contrarian flip** | "Most teams do [X]. We do [Y]. Here's why." | Thought-leadership |
| **Story beat** | "Then [event]. I realized [insight]." | Narrative |
| **Data + interpretation** | "[Number]. That means [implication]." | Authority |

Each interior slide ships with one **proof block** below the headline:
- Step slides: terminal block (3-line: command → progress → success)
- List slides: icon row + claim + numeric proof
- Comparison slides: two cards (left muted ✗, right magenta ✓)
- Quote slides: blockquote with magenta left border 3px
- Story slides: small italic body in muted gray

## End-slide patterns (final slide)

Pick one. Don't combine.

### Question close
"Which [thing] would you ship first?"
**When:** You want comments. Best for community building.
**Example:** "Which agent would you wire up first — onboarding or QA?"

### DM trigger
"Comment '[WORD]' — I'll DM the [asset]."
**When:** Lead magnet. ManyChat-friendly.
**Example:** "Comment 'STACK' — I'll DM the 4-agent doc we use."

### Soft offer
"We help [who] do [outcome]. DM '[word]' if [fit]."
**When:** B2B service. Looks like service announcement.
**Example:** "We embed AI agents inside creative teams. DM 'EMBED' if that's yours."

### Manifesto close
[No CTA. One line. Mic drop.]
**When:** Brand-building, thought leadership. Builds reputation > leads.
**Example:** "The next decade of creative work belongs to teams who build their own teammates."

## PAS / AIDA / SLAP frameworks mapped to slides

### PAS (Problem → Agitate → Solve) — 4 slides minimum
1. **Problem** — name the pain
2. **Agitate** — make it worse with stats
3. **Solve** — your approach
4. **CTA** — named ask

### AIDA (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action) — 5 slides minimum
1. **Attention** — hook from the 12
2. **Interest** — slide-2 pattern (promise/agitate)
3. **Desire** — proof block (case study, screenshot)
4. **Desire continued** — second proof
5. **Action** — named CTA

### SLAP (Stop → Look → Act → Purchase) — 6 slides minimum
1. **Stop** — visual/textual interrupt
2. **Look** — define the territory (who this is for)
3. **Look continued** — agitate or promise
4. **Act** — the steps/items
5. **Act continued** — more steps
6. **Purchase** — named CTA

## Voice tics (apply across all copy)

- **Sentence-fragment punch.** "Then it clicked." "Wrong." "Every time."
- **Odd numbered counts.** "9 agents" beats "10."
- **Self-aware aside.** "(you'll hate slide 4)" / "this part is unsexy but read it."
- **First-person stakes.** "I lost a $40k retainer the week I shipped this."
- **Mid-sentence em-dash reveal** — used sparingly. ("We tested 14 prompts — only 2 survived.")
- **Specificity.** "$11,400" beats "around $10k." "Tuesday 9:43am" beats "last week."
- **Jargon followed by translation.** "MCP servers — basically USB-C for AI tools."
- **Repetition for rhythm.** "Not the model. Not the prompt. The workflow."
- **One contraction per slide minimum** — kills the AI cadence.
- **Imperative verbs in step headlines.** INSTALL / ADD / LOAD / CONNECT.
